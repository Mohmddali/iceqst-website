Call for contributions

Thematic Topics

We welcome poster contributions across the following areas of emerging quantum science and technology. Tap any topic to read a short overview.

The broad frontier where quantum theory is turned into working technology. This track spans the convergence of quantum information, materials, optics and engineering, and welcomes cross-cutting or interdisciplinary work that bridges fundamental science and real devices — including advances that don't sit neatly inside a single sub-field.

Using qubits, superposition and entanglement to process information in ways classical machines cannot. Covers gate-model and measurement-based computing, quantum error correction, and the near-term (NISQ) hardware and applications being explored today across superconducting, trapped-ion and photonic platforms.

Exploiting quantum mechanics to transmit information with security rooted in the laws of physics rather than computational hardness. Includes quantum key distribution (QKD), entanglement distribution, quantum repeaters and networks, and the longer-term vision of a quantum internet.

The study of light and light–matter interaction down to the single-photon level. Underpins photonic qubits, single-photon sources and detectors, nonlinear and integrated photonics, and much of the enabling hardware for both quantum communication and computing.

Using quantum states to measure physical quantities with precision beyond classical limits. Applications include atomic clocks, gravimeters, magnetometers (such as nitrogen-vacancy centres in diamond) and quantum-enhanced imaging — turning fragile quantum coherence into practical measurement advantage.

Designing algorithms that use quantum resources for computational advantage — from Shor and Grover to variational and quantum machine-learning methods. Explores where quantum models can accelerate optimisation, simulation and learning, and how to benchmark genuine advantage over classical approaches.

Investigating materials whose behaviour emerges from quantum effects — superconductors, topological insulators, two-dimensional materials and spin systems — and the devices built from them, including qubit platforms, detectors and quantum-enabled electronics.

Examining the conceptual and mathematical bedrock of quantum theory: measurement, entanglement, nonlocality, contextuality and the competing interpretations. Increasingly, foundational insight feeds directly back into practical protocols across quantum information and metrology.
